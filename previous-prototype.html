HOW CLARITY SHOULD WORK

A standard that applies to everyone.

Clarity should not decide which political side is right. It should test what can be demonstrated, while separating fact, prediction, opinion and interpretation.

1. Identify atomic claims

Break each story into distinct, testable statements. Preserve the exact wording, dates, population and context of the claim.

2. Seek independent evidence

Prioritise primary documents, official datasets, court rulings, direct transcripts and robust research. Show original links and the date accessed. A link that merely repeats the claim is not independent verification.

3. Assign a specific status

Supported: the linked evidence substantiates the claim. Misleading: the underlying statement selectively presents or omits material context. Contradicted: credible evidence disputes it. Unverified: available evidence is inadequate. Opinion: not a straightforward factual proposition.

4. Assess headlines and framing separately

Record sensational wording, cherry-picked time periods, absent denominators and omitted context independently of factual accuracy. Strong language is not automatically false.

5. Never fake precision

An overall claim-support percentage can be computed from reviewed, checkable claims, but it is not a scientific probability that an entire article is true. Give the claim count, any exclusions and a confidence/coverage warning. Do not score a story with insufficient evidence.

6. Keep a public record

Publish evidence links, claim-by-claim explanations, timestamps, methodology changes, reader challenges and corrections. Apply one rubric across all outlets and viewpoints.

What remains to build for a real launch

Live publisher feeds and licensing; compliant article-text ingestion; search/retrieval; AI claim extraction; source cross-checks; editorial review; storage and accounts; automated updates and correction logging. None of those external services are secretly simulated here.
